Tone out of glyph density

Sixty-eight glyphs, from a bare space to $, ordered by how much of the cell each one inks. Adjacent steps differ by about one and a half percent of brightness, which is why a cheekbone survives. At arm's length the punctuation reads as a photograph; up close it reads as text.

It wants 140–200 columns. Fewer than that and consecutive glyphs sit too close for the eye to separate, so the face turns to speckle. Every glyph is ordinary text. The six charsets compared: home page.
